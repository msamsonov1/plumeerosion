Plume Surface Interaction (PSI) Analytical Mechanics

When a lunar descent engine fires near the vacuum surface, compressible supersonic exhaust impinges on cohesionless granular regolith. The resulting flow regime transitions from a supersonic free jet to a normal shock disk, high stagnation pressure zone, and supersonic outward radial wall jet generating shear forces and subsurface pressure gradients.

1. Plume Stagnation & Outward Wall Jet Dynamics

Gas Dynamics

As altitude $h$ decreases below the nozzle exit diameter ratio ($h/D_e < 8$), a normal shock forms above the lunar plane. Stagnation pressure $P_0(r)$ decays radially according to the Roberts-Adler self-similar formulation:

$$P(r, h) = P_{\text{stag}} \cdot \exp\left( -k \left( \frac{r}{h} \right)^2 \right), \quad P_{\text{stag}} \approx \frac{\gamma + 1}{2\gamma} \rho_{\text{jet}} v_{\text{exit}}^2 \left(\frac{D_e}{h}\right)^2$$

Outward radial gas velocity $v_r(r)$ reaches peak shear in the boundary wall jet layer at $r \approx 0.8 h \tan \theta_{\text{div}}$:

$$v_r(r) \approx v_{\text{exit}} \sqrt{1 - \left(\frac{P(r)}{P_{\text{stag}}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}}, \quad \tau_w = \frac{1}{2} C_f \rho_{\text{gas}}(r) v_r(r)^2$$
Key Metric: Shear stress $\tau_w$ exceeds 140 Pa at touchdown altitudes, initiating catastrophic sheet scouring.

2. Vacuum Fluidization & Regolith Scouring Inception

Soil Mechanics

Lunar regolith resists displacement via cohesive interparticle van der Waals forces and internal friction angle $\phi \approx 42^\circ$. Erosion initiates when modified vacuum Shields parameter $\theta^*$ surpasses the critical threshold $\theta_c$:

$$\theta^* = \frac{\tau_w}{(\rho_p - \rho_g) g_{\text{moon}} d_{50} + \frac{6 c}{\pi d_{50}}} \ge \theta_c \approx 0.045$$

Simultaneously, gas diffusing into the porous regolith creates upward vertical pressure gradients during plume transit, triggering deep explosive bulk fluidization when:

$$-\frac{\partial P}{\partial z} \ge (1 - \phi_p)(\rho_p - \rho_g) g_{\text{moon}}$$
Vacuum Effect: Zero ambient atmospheric backpressure amplifies upward gas escape gradients by $10^5 \times$.
